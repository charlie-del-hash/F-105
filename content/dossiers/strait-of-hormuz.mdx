The strait connects the Gulf to the Gulf of Oman and the open Indian Ocean. Every tanker loading at Ras Tanura, Kharg, Fujairah's inner terminals or Ras Laffan passes through it, in one of two lanes each two nautical miles wide, separated by a buffer, within Omani and Iranian waters.

## Why the plot matters

The Plot panel in the Bridge layout draws the strait schematically: the two lanes, a handful of tracks, and a datum where a mine or an incident has been reported. It is not a map. It is a way of seeing, at a glance, whether the flow through the lanes looks normal. The daily transit count (<Sym s="HORMUZ.TX" />) is the number behind the picture.

## The bypasses, and their limits

Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline and the UAE's line to Fujairah can move a few million barrels a day around the strait. There is no equivalent for Qatari LNG. This asymmetry is why a Hormuz scare shows up in <Sym s="JKM" /> and <Sym s="TTF" /> at least as sharply as in <Sym s="BRENT" />.

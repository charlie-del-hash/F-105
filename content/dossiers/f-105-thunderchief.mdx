The Thunderchief — "Thud" to everyone who flew or fixed it — was the largest single-seat, single-engine combat aircraft in history when it entered service, and it remained so. It was conceived as a nuclear strike fighter and ended as the conventional workhorse of the air war over North Vietnam.

## Design

Republic's Alexander Kartveli drew a heavy, mid-wing aircraft around an internal weapons bay and a very large turbojet. The forward-swept intake ramps in the wing roots are the recognition feature; the area-ruled fuselage and the all-moving tail gave it its speed. Later D models carried a bomb load on external pylons that would have been respectable for a medium bomber.

## Combat

From 1965 the Thud flew the majority of USAF strike sorties into North Vietnam from Thai bases, with losses concentrated in the 1965–68 period of Rolling Thunder. The two-seat F models, converted with radar-homing receivers and anti-radiation missiles, pioneered the Wild Weasel role; the G model was the definitive version.

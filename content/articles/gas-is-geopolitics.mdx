Three prices matter. <Sym s="HH" /> is the North American benchmark: cheap, isolated, and only connected to the world by the liquefaction plants on the Gulf coast. <Sym s="TTF" /> is Europe's: a virtual hub in the Netherlands whose price now sets the marginal cost of power across the continent. <Sym s="JKM" /> is Asia's: the spot price of a cargo delivered to Japan or Korea, and the number that tells you which way the LNG fleet will sail.

## The spread is the story

When JKM trades above TTF, cargoes go east; when it trades below, Europe wins them. Everything else — storage fill, pipeline politics, a cold snap in Beijing — turns into a move in that spread. This is why a European gas desk watches a Qatari export terminal and a Japanese weather forecast with equal attention.

## The shipping leg

Around a fifth of the world's LNG leaves the Gulf through the Strait of Hormuz. It has never been closed to LNG; it has been threatened often enough that the market prices the possibility every time the region's politics move. The transit indicator (see the Bridge layout) is a gas indicator too.

VLCC: the premium is in the ballast leg

TD3C has firmed without a matching move in cargo volumes. Owners are pricing the risk of waiting empty inside the Gulf — the ballast leg — rather than the laden transit out.

Shipping desk brief
Bottom line

Rates firm on positioning risk rather than demand. Fades if transits normalise; spikes if any incident touches a laden VLCC.

The setup. The Middle East Gulf to China route (TD3C) is the benchmark for the largest crude tankers. The Worldscale rate has moved up while the number of fixtures has not. That pattern usually means owners are charging for uncertainty, not for tonnage.

The mechanism. A VLCC waiting to load inside the Gulf is exposed for days. If the transit count (HORMUZ.TX) wobbles, owners want to be paid for that exposure before they commit a ship to a ballast run in. Charterers, who need the oil, pay.

The trade in words. Long the rate while the indicator stays noisy; flat the moment it settles for a fortnight. The time-charter equivalent (VLCC.TCE) tells you when owners are being paid enough to stop worrying.
